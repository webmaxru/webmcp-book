Tools, Not Clicks

Building Agent-Ready Websites with WebMCP

A book about WebMCP, the proposed web standard that lets websites hand AI agents real tools: named, described, schema-typed functions the browser's agent calls directly, inside the user's own session, with the human watching and in control.

Contents

17 chapters across 6 parts, plus 4 appendices · detailed outline

Part I

Why the Web Needs an Agent Interface

4 chapters
  1. 01 The Agentic Web Arrives Agents now act inside browsers on users’ behalf. This chapter shows why today’s approach, “actuation” through screenshots and DOM scraping, is slow, costly and fragile, and why sites have a stake in fixing it. Read · 30 min
  2. 02 From APIs to Tools: A Short History The path from REST and OpenAPI to LLM function calling, ChatGPT Actions and agent-to-agent protocols, which explains why “tools” became the unit of agent capability. Read · 26 min
  3. 03 MCP in Practice A working understanding of Anthropic’s Model Context Protocol: hosts, clients and servers over JSON-RPC, and what servers and clients offer each other. This is the vocabulary WebMCP borrows. Read · 43 min
  4. 04 Enter WebMCP Where WebMCP came from: community experiments such as MCP-B, a joint Microsoft and Google explainer, and incubation in the W3C Web Machine Learning Community Group. Also what it deliberately is not. Read · 33 min
Part II

The WebMCP API in Depth

4 chapters
  1. 05 Registering Tools with document.modelContext The core imperative API: registering a tool with a name, title, description, JSON Schema input and an async execute callback, and removing it again. Read · 42 min
  2. 06 Annotations, Lifecycle and Errors How the four annotation hints shape agent behaviour, what happens step by step when a tool is called, and how failures and cancellation reach the caller. Read · 45 min
  3. 07 Frames, Origins and Page-Embedded Agents WebMCP beyond the top-level page: Permissions Policy gating, exposing tools to specific origins, and discovering and executing tools from an agent that lives in the page or in an extension. Read · 37 min
  4. 08 The Declarative API: Forms as Tools Turning existing HTML forms into tools with attributes, and the events, submit handling and CSS hooks that support it. The chapter is explicit that this part has been taken out of the spec and is being explored separately. Read · 41 min
Part III

Building Agent-Ready Apps

3 chapters
  1. 09 Your First WebMCP App, End to End A full walkthrough: enable the flag or origin trial, add tools to a small app as a progressive enhancement, and drive it with Chrome’s inspector extension and a test agent. Read · 39 min
  2. 10 WebMCP in Frameworks Production patterns for component-based apps: tool registration tied to component and DI lifecycles, shared state, and routing. Read · 39 min
  3. 11 Designing Tools Agents Can Actually Use Tool design as product design: how many tools, how coarse, how to name and describe them, and what to return so an LLM picks the right one and uses it correctly. Read · 49 min
Part IV

Trust: Security, Privacy and Consent

2 chapters
  1. 12 Threat Models for Tool-Exposing Pages The attack surface WebMCP creates for sites, agents and users, mapped to the spec’s own threat analysis and to concrete mitigations a team can apply today. Read · 34 min
  2. 13 Privacy, Consent and the Human in the Loop Who approves what, and when. This chapter covers confirmation for consequential actions, the unresolved elicitation design, authenticated sessions, accessibility, and how to build UX that keeps users in control. Read · 28 min
Part V

Shipping and the Ecosystem

2 chapters
  1. 14 Testing, Debugging and Evals How to know your tools work when the caller is probabilistic: DevTools support, protocol-level testing, eval suites and measurement in production. Read · 50 min
  2. 15 Browser Support and the Standards Road Who implements what, how a Community Group draft becomes (or doesn’t become) a standard, and how to read origin trials, standards positions and TAG reviews. Read · 36 min
Part VI

Strategy and the Future

2 chapters
  1. 16 Product and Business Implications What agent-readiness means for product strategy: which journeys to expose first, brand control versus aggregation, conversion and attribution, and how WebMCP and backend MCP fit into one plan. Read · 34 min
  2. 17 The Road Ahead The open questions that will decide what WebMCP becomes, and how to build today so that your investment survives either outcome. Read · 32 min
